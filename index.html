The Framework

The Four Adult Attachment Styles

Adult attachment theory — pioneered by John Bowlby and expanded by Mary Ainsworth and later researchers — describes four patterns that emerge from early caregiving experiences. These patterns shape how we seek closeness, handle conflict, and respond to vulnerability in adult relationships. Importantly, they're not fixed: attachment styles can shift with awareness, new experiences, and healthy relationships.

🟢

Secure

Comfortable with both closeness and independence. Trusts others, communicates needs directly, and handles conflict constructively without fear of abandonment.

🟠

Preoccupied

High anxiety, low avoidance. Craves intimacy but fears rejection. May come across as clingy, emotionally intense, or in need of frequent reassurance.

🔵

Dismissing

Low anxiety, high avoidance. Deeply values self-sufficiency and may feel uncomfortable with emotional intimacy. Can come across as distant or emotionally unavailable.

🔴

Disorganized

High anxiety AND high avoidance. Simultaneously wants and fears closeness. May display confusing, contradictory behavior — craving connection but pulling away when it arrives.
